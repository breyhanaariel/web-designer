# 🐾 Poppy & Paws — Small-Business Website Redesign

> **Self-Directed Concept Redesign** — Poppy & Paws is a fictional local pet-grooming business created to demonstrate before/after website-redesign thinking. No real client, traffic, booking data, reviews, or business results are claimed.

**Website type:** Local small-business service website  
**Role:** Web Designer · Information Architecture · Visual Redesign · Conversion & Accessibility Planning  
**Format:** Focused before/after case study

## 🌷 Why This Study Exists

The three flagship showcase projects demonstrate complete new website concepts. Poppy & Paws demonstrates a different skill that freelance clients often need: **taking an existing small-business website that is difficult to use and making the business easier to understand, trust, find, and contact.**

## 🩹 Before — Concept Problems

The fictional “before” site represents common small-business website problems rather than a real company’s site.

- Services are buried in long paragraphs with no pricing guidance.
- The main call to action competes with several equally styled links.
- Mobile visitors must scroll to find phone/contact information.
- Grooming packages are described inconsistently.
- Location/service-area information is vague.
- Page headings do not clearly match local search intent.
- Low-contrast decorative text and tiny controls create accessibility problems.
- The visual design feels generic and does not communicate the friendly boutique experience.

## ✨ After — Redesign Direction

The redesign reframes the site around the questions a prospective customer needs answered before booking:

1. **What services do you offer?** — clear grooming packages and starting-price guidance.
2. **Do you serve my pet and area?** — visible pet types, service area, and practical FAQs.
3. **Why should I trust you?** — calm trust messaging, process explanation, safety/care information, and a place for real reviews when available.
4. **How do I book?** — one primary booking/inquiry action repeated at useful decision points.
5. **Will this work on my phone?** — mobile-first hierarchy, readable type, larger controls, and concise content.

## 📈 Business Goal → Redesign Decision → Intended Impact

| Business goal | Redesign decision | Intended business impact |
|---|---|---|
| Generate more qualified inquiries | Make **Book a Grooming Visit** the primary CTA and collect pet/service details in the inquiry flow | Reduce back-and-forth and help the business receive better project-fit information upfront |
| Make services easier to compare | Convert long service paragraphs into scannable package cards with starting prices and inclusions | Help visitors understand options faster and self-select an appropriate service |
| Improve local discoverability | Use descriptive page headings, service-area language, useful FAQs, and clear business/service content | Give search engines and prospective customers clearer context about what the business offers and where |
| Build trust before contact | Add process, care standards, practical policies, and a future location for genuine testimonials | Reduce uncertainty without inventing social proof |
| Improve accessibility and mobile usability | Increase contrast, use semantic headings/forms, retain visible focus states, enlarge tap targets, and simplify mobile navigation | Make the website easier to use for a broader range of visitors and devices |
| Strengthen brand perception | Replace generic layout choices with a soft, pet-friendly visual system and consistent content hierarchy | Help the website feel aligned with a thoughtful boutique service rather than an interchangeable template |

These are **intended outcomes**, not measured performance claims.

## 🔎 SEO Fundamentals Demonstrated

- Descriptive service-focused page titles and headings
- Clear local/service-area language
- Logical heading hierarchy
- FAQ content based on real customer decision questions
- Human-readable URLs and navigation labels in a real implementation
- Useful service copy rather than keyword stuffing
- Space for accurate business details, hours, and contact information

## ♿ Accessibility Fundamentals Demonstrated

- Semantic landmarks and headings
- Labeled form controls
- Keyboard-visible focus states
- Sufficient text/background contrast targets
- Large touch targets
- No critical information conveyed by color alone
- Mobile reading order that matches visual order
- Reduced reliance on decorative text treatments for important information

## 💗 Before / After Summary

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Generic hero with several competing links | One clear service promise + primary booking CTA |
| Long service paragraphs | Scannable packages with starting-price context |
| Contact details buried near the footer | Contact/booking access at meaningful decision points |
| Weak local context | Clear service area + search-minded service language |
| Inconsistent visual hierarchy | Branded, responsive component system |
| Accessibility treated as cleanup | Accessibility fundamentals included in the redesign system |

## 🌱 What I Would Measure on a Real Client Redesign

If this were a real engagement, I would establish a baseline and compare outcomes such as:

- inquiry-form completion rate
- qualified inquiry volume
- clicks to call/book
- mobile engagement and abandonment
- organic landing-page traffic for relevant local/service queries
- accessibility issues before vs. after remediation

Those metrics are intentionally **not fabricated** for this portfolio concept.
